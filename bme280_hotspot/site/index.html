Temperature

°C or °F · BME280

Air temperature at the sensor. About 20–24 °C (68–75 °F) is comfortable indoors. The board's own electronics can make it read 1–2 °C warm.

Relative humidity

%RH · BME280

How much water vapour the air holds, as a share of the most it could hold at this temperature. 30–60 % is comfortable; below 30 % feels dry, and above 60 % feels muggy and helps mould grow. Breathing on the sensor makes it jump.

Pressure

hPa (inHg) · BME280

The weight of the air above the sensor. This is the actual pressure where the sensor sits, not corrected to sea level, so it reads lower than weather reports at higher altitude. Weather systems move it by a few hPa over hours: falling usually means clouds or rain on the way, rising means clearing. 1 hPa = 0.02953 inHg.

Light

lux · BH1750

How bright it is at the sensor. Roughly: under 10 lx is dark, 50–300 lx a normally lit room, 300–1,000 lx a bright office, and 10,000 lx or more daylight. Cover the sensor or shine a phone light on it to see it change.

TVOC

ppb · SGP30

Total volatile organic compounds: gases from cleaning products, paint, cooking, perfume and people, in parts per billion. Under 220 ppb is good, 220–660 ppb moderate (open a window), and above 660 ppb poor. The SGP30 measures relative to its own baseline, so readings settle over the first hours.

eCO₂

ppm · SGP30

"Equivalent CO₂": the SGP30's estimate of carbon dioxide worked out from the VOCs, not a direct CO₂ measurement. Outdoor air is about 420 ppm and the sensor never reports below 400. Indoors, under 1,000 ppm is fine; 1,000–2,000 ppm means the room is stuffy and needs fresh air.

Each sensor is named by the last four characters of its board ID and keeps its colour on every graph. When the brain is online it uploads every 10 seconds and this page shows those readings from anywhere. Connect brain needs Chrome or Edge on a laptop or desktop; live readings show only on the computer the brain is plugged into. Otherwise this page shows readings published about every 6 minutes by a computer running the bridge; GitHub Pages can add up to 10 minutes of caching, so expect readings to be 5–20 minutes old. Gaps mean a sensor or the brain was offline; no values are filled in.
